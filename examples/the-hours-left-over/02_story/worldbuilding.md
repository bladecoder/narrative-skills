# Worldbuilding

Vigil became globally dominant within a few years because it was not marketed as a stimulant. It was framed as a correction: eight reclaimed hours, cleaner cognition, no circadian drag, no sleep-related accidents, and massive economic expansion. Labor law, transit systems, schooling, emergency medicine, and domestic life reorganized around continuous wakefulness.

Cities adapted fast. Restaurants, clinics, logistics hubs, and public administration remained active twenty-four hours a day. Apartments got smaller because bedrooms no longer mattered. Service corridors multiplied under retail towers. Public walls became programmable ad surfaces. Licensed retinal overlays and civic guidance meshes made urban life feel frictionless, provided users stayed subscribed and compliant. People kept the vocabulary of night but lost the ritual of ending a day.

Nexora Therapeutics built its legitimacy on spotless safety language, predictive monitoring, and dynamic dosing updates pushed through certified municipal distribution hubs. Public trust remains high because the visible gains are real and because the city is physically wired into Nexora's maintenance ecosystem.

The hidden problem is physiological. The human brain still accumulates sleep pressure and still requires true cycles of memory sorting, emotional regulation, and dream-state processing. Vigil suppresses the outward command to sleep but does not eliminate the underlying need. As long-term users accumulate debt, fragments of REM-like experience rupture waking consciousness in brief, contagious-looking intrusions. In a cyberpunk city built on uninterrupted labor, even a biological correction becomes a systems failure.
