# Chapter 3 Review

## Summary

The chapter resolves the story through Martin's public release of the restoration protocol and the city's first mass return to sleep.

## What works

- The opening civic failure signals make the crisis legible before the central confrontation.
- Vera's final argument presents a serious moral counterweight.
- The city's descent into sleep is rendered as social surrender rather than spectacle.
- The closing image returns the story to the body and fulfills the premise cleanly.

## Main problems

- The physical struggle in the hub must stay brief to avoid changing the genre register.
- The ending should resist overexplaining the political aftermath.

## Secondary problems

- A small number of public images carry a lot of weight; they should remain chosen and specific rather than multiplied.

## Continuity risks detected

- Preserve that the restoration is fast but not tidy.
- Keep Ines active in the release without taking the protagonist's final responsibility away.

## Prioritized recommendations

1. Keep the confrontation compact.
2. Let the city images do the work of scale.
3. End on bodily stillness rather than institutional consequence.
