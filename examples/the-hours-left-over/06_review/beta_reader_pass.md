# Beta Reader Pass

## Scope read

Full short story, chapters 1-3.

## General reading experience

The story grabbed me quickly because the premise became concrete almost immediately through the case interview instead of through a lecture. I understood the world, the problem, and the protagonist's role fast enough to relax into the tension. The ending felt inevitable in a good way.

## What made me want to keep reading

- Dalia's corridor description is an excellent early hook.
- Martin's first-person voice is precise and readable without sounding flat.
- The shift from private anomaly to public crisis is paced well across the three chapters.
- The final city imagery is strong and not overblown.

## Where attention dipped

- Chapter 2 carries a lot of explanation at once, though the intrusion scene keeps it alive.
- I briefly wondered whether Martin would become too passive because Ines brings in so much information, but chapter 3 fixes that.

## Most compelling elements

- The idea that sleep was not removed but displaced
- The corporate language that keeps trying to sanitize the horror
- The relief built into the final return to actual sleep

## Useful confusion versus frustrating confusion

### Useful confusion

- How quickly the restoration will spread
- How much Vera truly believes her own justification
- What exact dream images might have emerged in other people across the city

### Frustrating confusion to watch

- None serious. The science remains simplified, but in a way that fits the story form.

## Character response

- Martin is easy to follow because his professionalism and fear pull against each other.
- Ines feels competent and believable as a limited ally.
- Vera works because she sounds like someone who has spent years justifying irreversible decisions.

## Ending response

The final scene landed well. It was sad, relieving, and eerie at once. The line of ordinary people asking basic questions about sleep after the update felt especially effective.

## Reader-facing priorities

1. Keep the ending quiet.
2. Preserve the physical texture of the intrusions.
3. Do not add extra aftermath unless the story is expanded into something longer.
