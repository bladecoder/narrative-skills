# Partial Manuscript Review

## Scope reviewed

Chapters 1-3, with chapter 2 in revised form.

## Overall assessment

The manuscript delivers a coherent short science-fiction story with a clear conceptual hook, strong escalation, and a decisive ending. It remains intimate despite its social scale because Martin's professional voice anchors every development in lived perception and ethical pressure.

## What works at manuscript level

- The world premise becomes legible quickly without front-loading explanation.
- Dream intrusions escalate in a controlled sequence from testimony to personal fracture to public collapse.
- Martin's arc from technician to saboteur is earned through accumulating complicity rather than sudden heroism.
- The ending restores sleep in a way that feels costly, necessary, and thematically aligned.

## Main systemic risks

- The scientific logic must remain clear enough for reader trust without overburdening the story with explanation.
- The public-scale consequences could become abstract if the city images were less specific than they currently are.

## Secondary or local risks

- Vera works well as an institutional antagonist, but she should remain a worldview rather than a melodramatic villain.
- Ines is effective in the middle and end; any future expansion should decide whether to deepen her personal stakes or keep her function sharply tactical.

## Structure and pacing observations

- Chapter 1 establishes mood and premise efficiently.
- Chapter 2 carries the largest explanatory load and needed the revision pass it received.
- Chapter 3 resolves the ethical conflict without overstaying in aftermath.

## Character arc observations

- Martin's voice evolves from controlled processing to bodily surrender in a clean line.
- The story's emotional core works because the return of sleep is presented not as nostalgia but as biological truth.

## Continuity and coherence notes

- The restoration protocol is costly enough to avoid feeling like an easy fix.
- The story does not overclaim what happens after the final scene, which protects plausibility.

## Recommended next actions

1. Keep the manuscript scope as a complete short story.
2. Export the accepted draft for reader-facing formats.
3. Use the project as an English-language workflow fixture.
